Bird Outing

Privacy

Bird Outing — last updated 10 October 2026

Short version: the app has no account, no analytics, no advertising and no server of its own. Your outings, tracks and notes stay on your phone unless you export them. To answer your questions, the app asks the services listed below directly; some of those requests include your launch point.

What stays on your phone

Everything the app knows about you:

  • Your launch point — the address or place you set off from.
  • Your outings: when you started and ended, where you were, which birds you tagged and when, how many frames you took and how many you kept.
  • Your track during an outing, if you allow location access.
  • Your settings, including your eBird API key if you paste one.

All of it is stored on the device. There is no account to sign into, nothing is backed up to us, and we cannot see any of it. If you delete the app it goes with it.

Location

The app asks for location while you are using it, for two things: recording where you stood during an outing, so a spot that produced keepers can be told apart from one you only walked past; and noticing when you have travelled far enough from your launch point that it is worth asking whether to move it.

During an outing you started, the app keeps recording your track with the screen off, so a morning with the phone in a pocket still has one; the blue location indicator shows while it does, and it stops when you end the outing or after 12 hours. It does not track you at any other time. You can decline location access, and everything except the outing track still works.

None of these requests carries an identifier for your device. eBird requests carry your own key, which is tied to your eBird account.

Tapping Directions hands the destination to Maps, Google Maps or Waze, which apply their own privacy policies. Send to eBird opens eBird's own page; you sign in and submit there, and the app never sees your eBird password.

Your eBird key

The key is yours, obtained free from eBird. It is stored on your phone and sent only to eBird. We never see it. eBird's terms ask that you not share your key with anyone, and the app is built so that you never have to — it uses yours, or it does without.

The bird data

The historical figures the app ships with are summaries fitted from the eBird Basic Dataset (Version: EBD_relJul-2026, Cornell Lab of Ornithology, Ithaca, New York, Jul 2026), used for research and education on a non-commercial basis. They are derived statistics, not eBird records, and they contain nothing about any individual observer.

Children

The app is not directed at children and collects nothing from anyone.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it.
