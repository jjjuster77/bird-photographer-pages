Getting started

  1. Set a launch point. The first screen asks where you set off from. Every other number in the app follows from it — sunrise, drive times, which sites are even in reach.
  2. Set your outing. On the Plan tab, pick the day and the hours you can give it, and confirm. Until that is set the app will not rank anything, because there is nothing honest to rank against.
  3. Pick your bird. The ranking is for one target species, with companions alongside.

Common questions

The plan is empty. Either the window has not been confirmed — the Plan tab says so — or nothing within your drive cap clears the crowding limit. Widen the drive, allow more company, or push the home-by time later.

The weather chips are dotted and won't press. The forecast reaches about a week out. Past that there is nothing to price them against, so they go inert rather than pretending.

Do I need an eBird API key? No. The planner's odds come from bundled historical data. A key — free from ebird.org/api/keygen, pasted under the gear icon — adds recent sightings at a site and lets the app refresh how busy places are.

A site says "sunrise assumed". About one site in eight has a published opening time. For the rest the app assumes sunrise and tells you it is assuming, rather than inventing a gate.

The odds look high / low for a place I know. They are rates over past mornings from other people's checklists, not a prediction about today. A quiet hour is not an empty one, and a good spot nobody reports is invisible to the app. The About screen, under the gear icon, explains what the numbers are and are not.

Which owl / which songbird? Tap the group. Owls and songbirds are rolled up, with the breakdown one tap underneath.

Reporting a problem

Say what you were doing, which tab you were on, and the version from the bottom of the About screen. Screenshots help more than anything.
